# Baseline findings — 15 August 2026

This report records the first equal-language quality pass. Generated response
artifacts remain local and ignored because they are run outputs, not fixtures.

## Baseline

- Configuration: `gpt-5.6-terra`, low reasoning effort.
- Coverage: all 36 source/target directions; each CEFR level appeared six times.
- Structural result: 36/36 passed.
- Developer review: meaning, quantities, chronology, and causal relationships
  were generally preserved; no clear invented event or material factual
  omission was found.

The review flagged three repeated low-level editing problems:

- German A2 assigned movement to batteries rather than buses.
- Turkish A2 produced the non-standard form `Müdüreye`.
- French A1 preserved a quotation through an awkward literal structure.

Italian-to-Spanish A1 also softened “investigations” to “news” in one run. This
was not a structural failure but was recorded as a nuance-preservation risk.

## Focused comparisons

The flagged German, Turkish, and French cases were rerun under bounded changes.

| Configuration | Observation |
| --- | --- |
| Terra, medium | Fixed the German semantic role once, but Turkish and French issues persisted. |
| Terra, medium + edit instruction | Fixed Turkish; German remained nondeterministic and French remained mechanical. |
| Sol, low + edit instruction | Natural output, but added an unsupported “online” detail in Turkish and paraphrased the French quotation more freely. |
| Sol, medium + edit instruction | Correct, natural, and closest to the source across all three focused cases. |

## Decision

Use `gpt-5.6-sol` with medium reasoning effort for the quality-validation phase.
Sol costs twice as much per text token as Terra, but the focused comparison found
a material multilingual editing gain on the product's core behavior. Revisit the
trade-off after collecting human labels and production-shaped examples.

## Final candidate run

- Configuration label: `sol-medium-v1`.
- Coverage: all 36 directions with six cases at every CEFR level.
- Structural result: 36/36 passed.
- The previously flagged German, Turkish, and French failures did not recur.
- Developer review found no clear invented event, changed quantity, or material
  omission.

Two nuance risks remain for human review: “investigations” occasionally softened
to broader “reporting”, and “published” was sometimes rendered as “published
online”. Same-language C1/C2 outputs also need proficient reviewers to confirm
that increased complexity remains natural rather than cosmetic.

This is a developer review, not a substitute for the human scorecard. At least
one proficient reviewer per target language should label the generated CSV
before making a public CEFR-quality claim.
